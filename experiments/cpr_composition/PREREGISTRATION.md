# Pre-registration — Group Composition and Governance in a Common-Pool Resource Game

**Status: DRAFT. Not registered. No data collected. No ethics approval obtained.**

This document is version-controlled alongside the code that generates its
numbers. Every quantity below is reproduced by running the modules in this
folder; nothing is asserted that the code does not compute.

---

## 0. What changed from the first draft, and why

The simulation was run before the design was fixed. It found five things:

| # | Finding | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The "sustainable" comparison policy was `S/N`: below S = N·cap it requests the **whole stock**, above it the cap truncates every request and it **is all-max**. Neither branch is restraint. | Replaced with the exact fixed-point harvest (`cpr_game.sustainable_total`). The two arms are now different arms. |
| 2 | The mechanical composition slope is **−0.19 to −0.24**, which is the SESOI H2 pre-registered as its effect of interest. | H2 tested against the mechanical baseline, not against zero. See §5. |
| 3 | The mechanical composition curve is a **step, not a line** — survival at 0–1 maximisers, collapse at 2+. | Threshold specification pre-registered alongside the linear one. |
| 4 | Participant numbers were internally inconsistent: 720 participants ↔ 240 groups requires groups of 3. | 240 groups × 4 = **960 participants**, screening pool **1440**. See §3. |
| 5 | The equivalence test compared an **unstandardised** coefficient against a **standardised** SESOI. | Both standardised before testing (`analysis_plan.primary_analysis`). |

Finding 2 is the one that would have mattered most. Against a null of zero,
simulated data generated by pure arithmetic returns "H2 strongly supported."

## 1. Hypotheses

**H1 (governance, confirmatory).** Final stock is ordered G2 > G1 > G0, where
G0 = no communication, G1 = communication, G2 = communication + costly
sanctions. Directional. Tested as two contrasts against G0.

**H2 (composition, confirmatory, primary).** Groups with more high-D members
have lower final stock **beyond what their extraction arithmetic already
implies**.

- Estimand: standardised slope of `S_final/K` on `n_high_d`.
- Null: **the mechanical baseline slope at the realised parameters**, converted
  to standardised units (`analysis_plan.standardized_baseline`), *not* zero.
- SESOI: 0.20 standardised units either side of that baseline.
- **Supported** if the 90% CI lies entirely below `baseline − 0.20`.
- **Falsified** if the 90% CI lies entirely inside `baseline ± 0.20`
  (equivalence), or entirely above `baseline + 0.20` (wrong sign).
- **Inconclusive** if the CI crosses a band edge. This is a power statement,
  reported as such, not written up as a null result.

**H3 (interaction, exploratory).** The composition effect is smaller under G2
than G0. **Declared underpowered in advance** — an interaction needs roughly
four times the sample of a main effect of the same size, and this design has
240 groups, not 960. No confirmatory claim will be made from it, regardless of
what it shows.

## 2. Construct validity of the composite — checked, not assumed

`D_score` averages z-scores of SDO-dominance, risk preference (Dohmen SOEP item
plus an incentivised lottery), and delay discounting (Kirby MCQ). That average
is only meaningful if the three covary.

**Pre-registered check:** `design.composite_coherence` on the screening sample,
reporting all three pairwise correlations and Cronbach's alpha.

- Mean inter-item r ≥ 0.20 → proceed with the composite as planned.
- Mean inter-item r < 0.20 → **the composite is abandoned**, and the three
  measures enter the model as separate predictors with a Holm correction
  across the three. This fallback is registered now precisely so it cannot be
  chosen later on the basis of which version gives a better result.

Naming note: "high-D" is a label for the top tercile of this composite. It is
not a personality type, a diagnosis, or a claim about anyone outside this
sample, and it will not be described as one in any write-up.

## 3. Design and sample size

| quantity | value | source |
|---|---|---|
| group size | 4 | fixed |
| compositions per arm | 0,1,2,3,4 high-D, balanced | `design.assign_groups` |
| governance arms | G0, G1, G2 | fixed |
| groups per arm | 80 | power, below |
| **total groups** | **240** | `design.screening_pool_required(80)` |
| **participants** | **960** | 240 × 4 |
| **screening pool** | **1440** | high-D supply is binding |

**Why the screening pool is 1.5× the participant count.** Balancing
compositions 0–4 equally makes the mean 2 high-D members per group, so half of
all participants must be high-D, while a top-tercile screen supplies a third.
The multiple is not slack; it is forced by the design.

**Power** (`design.power_ols`, normal approximation, 5 predictors):

| groups | power at β = 0.20 | power at β = 0.30 |
|---|---|---|
| 120 | 0.57 | 0.89 |
| 180 | 0.75 | 0.98 |
| **240** | **0.86** | **1.00** |

203 groups are needed for 80% power at β = 0.20. The draft's 180 groups gives
0.75 — which is why the group count, not the participant count, is the number
the design is built from.

The normal approximation is adequate for design arithmetic and is **not** a
simulation-based power analysis of the actual estimator. Before registration, a
simulation-based power check should be run using `cpr_game.simulate` with a
behavioural noise model.

## 4. Procedure

**Stage 1 (screen, N ≈ 1440):** SDO-7 dominance subscale, risk items, Kirby
MCQ, demographics. Composite computed, coherence checked (§2), top tercile
flagged.

**Stage 2 (main, N = 960):** Block randomisation into 240 groups crossed by
composition × governance, from a **recorded seed** (`design.assign_groups`).
The seed is registered with this document so the assignment is auditable.

**Game:** S0 = 50, K = 100, g from the pilot (§6), cap = 8, T = 20 rounds.
Each round: requests → rationing if demand ≥ stock → regeneration. Collapse is
absorbing. G1/G2 chat before rounds 1, 6, 11, 16. G2 sanctions cost the
sanctioner 1 token and remove 3 from the target, **settled against tokens, not
against the stock** — a sanction that removed resource units would confound
punishment with extraction, and the stock is the DV.

**Payment:** show-up fee $6.00 plus $0.05 per token.

**The show-up fee is the subsidy term.** `cpr_game.subsidy_ratio` reports a
coupling index of 0.57 at default parameters: 43% of maximum earnings arrive
regardless of what happens to the stock. The experiment is, by construction, a
model of *subsidised* extraction — hyperpredation in the taxonomy of
`extraction_dynamics/` — and not of subsistence. This is stated as a scope
condition, not buried as a limitation.

## 5. Analysis

**Unit:** group (N = 240). No participant-level test of a group-level claim.

**Primary (H2):** OLS of standardised `S_final/K` on standardised `n_high_d`,
governance dummies (G0 reference), and comprehension score. Equivalence test
against the mechanical baseline per §1.

**Primary (H1):** the two governance dummy coefficients, with a pre-registered
ordering contrast G2 > G1 > G0.

**Threshold specification (registered, secondary):** indicator for
`n_high_d ≥ cut`, reported for every cut. Because the location is selected by
fit, **the p-value from the winning split is not a p-value** and only the
effect size and the selection procedure will be reported.

**Secondary:** Cox model on rounds-to-collapse with survivors **censored at
T = 20** (a group that never collapses is not a group that collapsed at round
20); round-level mixed model of extraction with a random intercept per group
and a **fixed round trend** (extraction trends within a session regardless of
composition, and a model without the trend attributes it elsewhere); Gini of
within-group extraction; sanction frequency and targeting in G2.

**Exclusions, fixed in advance:** groups with any dropout before round 10;
groups with more than one participant scoring 0 on both comprehension items.
Exclusions are reported with results either way.

**Stopping rule:** fixed N. No interim analysis. No peeking.

## 6. Pilot

60 groups (20 per arm) for parameter tuning and attrition only. **No hypothesis
test is run on pilot data, and pilot data are not pooled with the main sample.**

The pilot's job is to set `g` inside the design window — the range where
maximisation collapses the stock and restraint does not, and where compositions
separate. `parameter_sweep.find_design_window` computes it. Outside that window
the study has no dependent variable: at low `g` every group collapses whatever
it does, at high `g` no group can collapse even taking the cap every round.
Both produce zero variance and zero power, and neither is a finding.

`parameter_sweep.defection_incentive` must also confirm that defection pays the
**defector** more at the chosen `g`. At the current parameters it does (160
tokens versus 40 under universal restraint), while the group total is *higher*
under restraint. Only the individual comparison is the dilemma; if the
individual incentive were absent, the study would be measuring comprehension
rather than dominance.

---

## 7. What this study cannot show

- Nothing about behaviour outside a paid 20-round laboratory game with a
  show-up fee. The external validity argument is not made here.
- Nothing causal about personality. Composition is randomised; the traits are
  measured, not manipulated.
- Nothing about the interaction (H3), which is underpowered by design.
- Nothing about the correlational claims that motivated this design. Those
  claims — that dominance orientation correlates with extraction rates at a
  societal scale — have no source, sample, or method attached, and this
  experiment is what it would take to generate one datum bearing on them. A
  20-round game with four undergraduates is not a civilisation.
